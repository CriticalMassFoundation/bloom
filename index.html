Your journal is yours.

Bloom privacy policy · updated 28 October 2026

What Bloom is

Bloom is a private journal for young people aged 10 to 18. It helps you understand yourself, your feelings and the biology that goes with them, and connects you to the world around you, one page at a time.

Why Bloom is free

Bloom is a pilot for the Critical Mass Foundation, which is being set up to make free digital tools that have a positive impact on the world. They are free from adverts, digital manipulation and harm. Bloom is not made by a company and makes no money from you, so it has no reason to collect anything about you.

The short version

Everything you write in Bloom stays on your phone. It is locked with a code that only you choose, and nobody can open it without that code: not us, not your school, not advertisers. Bloom has no accounts, no adverts and no tracking.

What Bloom keeps, and where

Your pages, feelings, notes, the name you chose, your colours and your city are saved only on this device. They are not sent to us or to anyone else. If you delete Bloom, they are deleted too, unless you made a backup.

Talking instead of typing

When you use the microphone, your phone turns your voice into words on the phone itself. Your voice is not sent anywhere. If your phone can't do that without the internet, Bloom doesn't offer it.

Your city and the weather

If you want, you type your city yourself; Bloom never uses your phone's location. To start today's page with the weather, Bloom asks Apple where that city is, and asks Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com), a free weather service, for that place's weather. Only the city's position is sent, never who you are or what you write. Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0).

Reminders

If you ask for a reminder, it is set on your phone. No server is involved.

Helping make Bloom better

Bloom counts which parts you use, like how often you open In season or save a page. It never counts or reads what you write. You can see every number in Make it yours, under "Help make Bloom better". After your fifth saved page, Bloom asks you once whether it may send these numbers to us. Nothing is sent unless you say yes, and you can turn it off there at any time. If you say yes, Bloom sends them at most once a day, and only on days you use it: how much each number grew since the last time, the date, Bloom's version, your age level and which gifts you have. It never sends your name, your city, your writing or anything that tells your phone apart from anyone else's, so we can't know whose numbers they are. They arrive in a spreadsheet the Critical Mass Foundation keeps with Google. Like any internet service, Google sees the internet address they came from, but we never keep or look at it. If you have allowed it in your phone's settings, Apple also shares overall app numbers with us, such as how many people use Bloom, but never anything about you or what you wrote.

What we never do

We never collect what you write, never sell or share data, never show adverts, never use tracking tools, and never contact anyone on your behalf.

Facts and feelings

The facts about seasons, animals, food and feelings are part of the app. Reading them sends nothing anywhere. What Bloom says about feelings and the body is for learning, not medical advice.

Help lines and links

The SOS page lists help lines in Norway. If you call one or open one of their websites, that service is not part of Bloom and has its own privacy rules. Bloom does not know whether you called or visited.

Your code

Bloom is locked with a 6-digit code that only you choose. Face ID or Touch ID can open it too, if your phone has them. Your code is kept only on this phone, never in a backup and never sent anywhere. Because we don't keep your journal, we can't open it for you: if you forget your code, nobody can.

A copy of your journal

If you delete Bloom, your journal is deleted too, unless you choose a folder for a copy. If you do, Bloom keeps two files there after every page you save: your book, laid out like your pages, and a backup to bring your journal back into Bloom. Both are locked with your code. If the folder is in iCloud Drive, it is stored in the iCloud of whoever owns this phone's Apple account, which might be a parent; they would still need your code to open it. A lock makes your journal much harder to open, but anyone who knows or guesses your code can, so keep it to yourself.

Children and young people

Bloom is made for ages 10–18. We collect no personal information. The numbers above, if you choose to send them, can't be traced back to you, so there is nothing about you for us to store, share or delete. A parent or guardian can ask us any question at the address below.

Who is responsible

Bloom is made by Abid Abou Alshamat as a pilot for the Critical Mass Foundation. Questions: hello@criticalmass.foundation

Our promise

Bloom is made by volunteers, without lawyers or a legal department. This page describes, as honestly as we can, what Bloom does and what we intend. We have tried hard to follow the rules that protect you, and we have no wish to trick anyone, collect more than we say, or take away any of your rights. If you think we have got something wrong, or that Bloom does something this page doesn't say, please tell us at hello@criticalmass.foundation. We will look at it seriously, answer you, and fix it as quickly as we can. You also always have the right to complain to Datatilsynet, the Norwegian Data Protection Authority (datatilsynet.no).

Changes

If this ever changes, we will update this page and the date at the top before the change happens.
